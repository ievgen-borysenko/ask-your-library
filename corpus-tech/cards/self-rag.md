# Self-RAG: Learning to Retrieve, Generate, and Critique through Self-Reflection — Akari Asai, Zeqiu Wu, Yizhong Wang, Avirup Sil, Hannaneh Hajishirzi

## Summary

Self-RAG, authored by Akari Asai, Zeqiu Wu, Yizhong Wang, Avirup Sil, and Hannaneh Hajishirzi (University of Washington, Allen Institute for AI, IBM Research AI), presents a framework called Self-Reflective Retrieval-Augmented Generation (Self-Rag) that addresses factual inaccuracies in large language models. Rather than retrieving passages indiscriminately, Self-Rag trains a single language model end-to-end to decide when retrieval is needed, generate text informed by retrieved passages, and critique both the retrieved evidence and its own outputs using special tokens called reflection tokens. These tokens cover four aspects: whether to retrieve, whether retrieved passages are relevant, whether outputs are supported by evidence, and whether outputs are useful. The framework enables controllable inference-time behaviour. Evaluated zero-shot across six tasks—open-domain QA, fact verification, multiple-choice reasoning, and long-form generation with citations—Self-Rag (7B and 13B parameters) outperforms larger LLMs and conventional retrieval-augmented models. The paper is organised as an introduction, framework description, experiments, results and analysis, and appendices covering training details, evaluation setup, and GPT-4 prompting instructions.

## Key ideas

- **Adaptive on-demand retrieval** — Instead of always retrieving a fixed number of passages, Self-Rag trains the LM to decide at each segment whether retrieval is necessary, unnecessary, or whether previously retrieved evidence can be reused. (3 Self-Rag: Learning to Retrieve, Generate and Critique)
- **Reflection tokens** — Four special token types (Retrieve, IsREL, IsSUP, IsUSE) are inserted into the generation stream to signal retrieval need, passage relevance, output support, and overall usefulness, making the model self-critiquing. (3 Self-Rag: Learning to Retrieve, Generate and Critique)
- **End-to-end training on reflection tokens** — The LM is trained to predict reflection tokens alongside ordinary text tokens, so critique and retrieval decisions are learned behaviours rather than post-hoc additions. (3 Self-Rag: Learning to Retrieve, Generate and Critique)
- **Inference-time controllability** — Because reflection tokens are explicit, users can adjust the model's behaviour at test time to favour, for example, higher citation accuracy or greater fluency, without retraining. (3 Self-Rag: Learning to Retrieve, Generate and Critique)
- **GPT-4-collected training signal** — Reflection token labels for training data are obtained by prompting GPT-4 with carefully designed instructions and demonstrations, one set per token type. (Appendix D Full List of Instructions and Demonstrations for GPT-4)
- **Superiority over conventional RAG and larger LLMs** — Across six zero-shot tasks, Self-Rag 7B and 13B significantly outperform standard retrieval-augmented models and proprietary models such as ChatGPT on most metrics. (5 Results and Analysis)
- **Low reliance on parametric memory** — Only 2% of Self-Rag's correct predictions are absent from retrieved passages, compared with 15–20% for instruction-tuned baselines, indicating stronger grounding in retrieved evidence. (Appendix C Results)
- **Segment-level generation** — The framework treats one sentence as a generation segment, applying retrieval and critique decisions at each segment rather than once per query, though any segment granularity is supported. (3 Self-Rag: Learning to Retrieve, Generate and Critique)

## Terms

- **Reflection tokens** — Special tokens added to the LM vocabulary that signal retrieval need (Retrieve), passage relevance (IsREL), output support level (IsSUP), and response usefulness (IsUSE), generated by the model itself during inference.
- **Retrieve token** — A reflection token with values {Yes, No, Continue} that determines at each segment whether the retriever should be called, skipped, or whether prior evidence should be reused.
- **IsREL token** — A reflection token with values {Relevant, Irrelevant} that judges whether a retrieved passage provides useful information for the given input.
- **IsSUP token** — A reflection token with values {Fully supported, Partially supported, No support / Contradictory} that assesses how much of the generated output is entailed by the retrieved evidence.
- **IsUSE token** — A reflection token scored on a five-point scale that evaluates the overall usefulness of a generated response to the input.
- **Parametric memory** — Knowledge encoded in the LM's weights during pre-training, as opposed to non-parametric knowledge retrieved at inference time from an external corpus.
- **Non-parametric memory** — Knowledge drawn from retrieved external passages at inference time, contrasted with knowledge stored in model parameters.

## Themes

- **Factuality and attribution** — The work is centrally concerned with reducing hallucination and ensuring generated claims are traceable to cited retrieved passages.
- **Selective versus indiscriminate retrieval** — A recurring contrast is drawn between always retrieving (which can introduce irrelevant content) and retrieving only when the generation genuinely requires factual grounding.
- **LM versatility preservation** — The framework is designed so that improving factuality does not sacrifice the model's ability to handle tasks that do not require retrieval.
- **Self-critique as a training objective** — Rather than relying on external verifiers, the model learns to evaluate its own outputs, embedding quality control within the generation process itself.
- **Controllable inference** — Reflection tokens serve as levers that allow task-specific tuning of model behaviour at test time without additional fine-tuning.
- **Evaluation breadth** — The work consistently emphasises holistic assessment across diverse task types (closed-set, short-form QA, long-form with citations) and multiple metrics (accuracy, factuality, fluency, citation precision and recall).
