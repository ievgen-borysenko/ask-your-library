# Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models — Jason Wei, Xuezhi Wang, Dale Schuurmans, Maarten Bosma, Brian Ichter, Fei Xia, Ed H. Chi, Quoc V. Le, Denny Zhou

## Summary

This 2022 paper by Jason Wei, Xuezhi Wang, Dale Schuurmans, and colleagues at Google Research, Brain Team, investigates how providing a few demonstrations of step-by-step reasoning in prompts—called chain-of-thought prompting—substantially improves the reasoning performance of large language models (LLMs). The authors argue that this capability is an emergent property of sufficiently large models and does not require any finetuning. Experiments across three large language models (LaMDA, GPT-3, and PaLM) on arithmetic, commonsense, and symbolic reasoning benchmarks show striking gains, including state-of-the-art results on the GSM8K math word problem benchmark using PaLM 540B with only eight exemplars. The paper is organised as a core empirical study across three reasoning domains, followed by discussion of limitations and emergence, related work, and extensive appendices providing full prompts, raw results, error analyses, and reproducibility details.

## Key ideas

- **Chain-of-thought prompting** — Providing a few input–output exemplars that include intermediate reasoning steps in the prompt significantly improves LLM performance on complex reasoning tasks without any finetuning. (2 Chain-of-Thought Prompting)
- **Emergent property of scale** — Chain-of-thought reasoning only reliably improves performance in models above roughly 10B parameters; it hurts smaller models, making it an emergent capability. (6 Discussion)
- **Arithmetic reasoning gains** — PaLM 540B with eight chain-of-thought exemplars achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on GSM8K, surpassing finetuned GPT-3 with a verifier. (3 Arithmetic Reasoning)
- **Commonsense reasoning applicability** — The linguistic nature of chain-of-thought reasoning makes it broadly applicable to commonsense tasks, including multi-hop strategy questions and robot action planning. (4 Commonsense Reasoning)
- **Out-of-domain length generalisation** — Chain-of-thought prompting enables models to generalise to symbolic reasoning problems with more steps than those seen in the few-shot exemplars. (5 Symbolic Reasoning)
- **External calculator augmentation** — Appending a Python-based external calculator to evaluate equations in generated chains of thought further boosts arithmetic accuracy on most benchmarks. (Appendix B All Experimental Results)
- **Correctness of generated chains** — Analysis of 50 correct-answer chains from LaMDA 137B on GSM8K found that 49 of 50 had correct logic and mathematics, with only one arriving at the right answer through flawed reasoning. (Appendix D Appendix: Additional Analysis)
- **Standard prompting as lower bound** — Standard few-shot prompting underestimates LLM capability; chain-of-thought prompting reveals higher performance on tasks where standard prompting shows flat scaling curves. (6 Discussion)

## Terms

- **Chain of thought** — A coherent series of intermediate natural-language reasoning steps generated by a model that leads to a final answer, provided as part of few-shot exemplars.
- **Chain-of-thought prompting** — A prompting method in which a few demonstrations pairing questions with explicit intermediate reasoning steps are given to a language model at inference time.
- **Standard prompting** — The baseline few-shot prompting approach in which exemplars contain only input–output pairs without intermediate reasoning steps.
- **Emergent property** — A capability that appears only at certain model scales and cannot be predicted by extrapolating performance from smaller models.
- **Out-of-domain (OOD) test set** — An evaluation set for symbolic reasoning tasks in which examples have more reasoning steps than those present in the few-shot exemplars.
- **External calculator** — A post-hoc Python-based tool applied to equations within generated chains of thought to correct arithmetic computation errors without altering the reasoning steps.

## Themes

- **Scale as a prerequisite for reasoning** — Across all three reasoning domains, meaningful chain-of-thought gains appear only in very large models, making model scale a recurring condition throughout the paper.
- **Prompting as an alternative to finetuning** — The work consistently frames chain-of-thought prompting as a way to unlock capabilities from off-the-shelf models, with no model weights updated at any point.
- **Generality across reasoning types** — The same prompting technique is evaluated on arithmetic, commonsense, and symbolic tasks, with the aim of demonstrating broad rather than task-specific applicability.
- **Faithfulness and factuality of generated reasoning** — The paper repeatedly notes that generated chains of thought are not always factually correct, treating this as a key limitation and caution against real-world deployment.
- **Robustness of the method** — Results are tested across multiple annotators, exemplar sets, and language model families to establish that gains are not artefacts of a particular prompt or model choice.
