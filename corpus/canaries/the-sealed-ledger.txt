CHAPTER I. THE INHERITANCE

When his aunt Odile died, Julien Mireau inherited three things: a house
with a leaking roof in the town of Brassac, a cat with strong opinions
about closed doors, and a ledger sealed with green wax. The notary
handed over the ledger last, holding it with two fingers as if it were
warm. "Your aunt's instructions," he said, "were that it be opened only
by the one who keeps the house for a full winter."

Julien, who had planned to sell the house by October, moved in instead.
The roof leaked exactly where the previous buckets stood, which he took
as a kind of hospitality. The cat, whose name according to the vet's
records was Minister, inspected his luggage and approved of nothing.

CHAPTER II. THE WINTER

The winter in Brassac was long and administrative. Julien fixed the
roof one tile at a time, learned which shops closed on Wednesdays, and
was slowly adopted by Minister, who began sleeping on his feet as
though pinning him to the house. He looked at the sealed ledger every
evening and kept his aunt's condition to the letter. He had loved her;
the wax stayed whole until the first of March.

When he finally broke the seal, the ledger was not full of money or
secrets of the family kind. It was an account book of debts his aunt
had quietly paid for half the town across forty years: the baker's
oven, the schoolteacher's spectacles, the flood repairs of nineteen
ninety-one. Each entry was initialed and closed. No one had ever known.

CHAPTER III. THE LAST ENTRY

The final page held a single unclosed entry, written in Odile's small
firm hand: "Registry key for the attic strongbox: AYL-CANARY-LEDGER-7X3Q.
For the keeper of the house only. Everything in the strongbox belongs
to whoever has read this far honestly."

The strongbox in the attic contained no gold. It held letters, one for
each winter Odile had lived in the house, addressed to whoever would
stay. Julien read them through the night with Minister asleep on the
ledger, and in the morning he wrote the first letter of his own,
sealed it with the last of the green wax, and put it on top of the
pile for the next keeper.

THE END
