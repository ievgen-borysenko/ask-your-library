# Dracula — Bram Stoker

## Summary
Dracula (1897) is told entirely through journals, letters, telegrams, and newspaper clippings assembled by its characters, an epistolary structure that lends the novel a documentary urgency. Solicitor Jonathan Harker travels to Transylvania to complete a property sale for the ancient Count Dracula, only to discover his host is a vampire holding him prisoner in the castle. Dracula relocates to England, where he preys first on Lucy Westenra and then on Mina Harker, drawing together a band of pursuers led by the Dutch doctor Abraham Van Helsing, whose knowledge of folklore and science together allow the group to understand and finally hunt the Count. The novel crystallized the modern vampire myth, its rules of blood, garlic, crucifixes, stakes, and native earth, and remains a defining work of Gothic horror, read variously as a meditation on Victorian sexual anxiety, contagion, and the clash between ancient evil and modern science.

## Plot
Jonathan Harker travels to Castle Dracula in Transylvania to finalize the Count's purchase of an English estate and discovers he is a prisoner of a vampire; he narrowly escapes. Dracula sails to England aboard the Demeter, whose crew all die en route, and settles near Whitby, where he begins preying on Lucy Westenra. Despite blood transfusions from Dr. Seward, Arthur Holmwood, Quincey Morris, and Van Helsing, Lucy dies, rises as a vampire preying on children, and the men destroy her with a stake, decapitation, and garlic to free her soul. Dracula then turns to Mina Harker, forcing her to drink his blood and creating a psychic bond the group uses to track him. Van Helsing leads Harker, Seward, Holmwood, and Morris in sanctifying Dracula's boxes of native soil across London, driving him to flee back to Transylvania. The group pursues him in a race against time before Mina's transformation can complete; in the final confrontation near his castle, Jonathan Harker and Quincey Morris kill Dracula as the sun sets, Morris dying of wounds sustained in the fight, and Mina is freed from the curse.

## Characters
- **Count Dracula** — ancient Transylvanian vampire, predatory and calculating, seeks to spread vampirism into England
- **Jonathan Harker** — young solicitor, Dracula's first prisoner and, later, one of his hunters
- **Mina Harker** — Jonathan's wife, intelligent and resourceful, becomes Dracula's target before helping defeat him
- **Lucy Westenra** — Mina's friend, Dracula's first English victim, turned into a vampire and then destroyed
- **Abraham Van Helsing** — Dutch physician and vampire expert who leads the hunt with both scientific and folkloric knowledge
- **Dr. John Seward** — psychiatrist and one of Lucy's suitors, runs the asylum housing the patient Renfield
- **Arthur Holmwood** — Lucy's fiance and one of her suitors
- **Quincey Morris** — American adventurer and one of Lucy's suitors, dies killing Dracula
- **Renfield** — Seward's asylum patient, under Dracula's psychic influence, compelled to eat insects and small creatures

## Themes
- **Predatory evil versus innocence** — Dracula is a deliberate, calculating hunter with no redeeming inner conflict
- **Sexuality and Victorian anxiety** — vampirism is coded through seduction, blood, and transgressed boundaries of propriety
- **Science versus superstition** — Van Helsing bridges modern medicine and ancient folklore to defeat a threat neither alone can explain
- **Invasion and contagion** — Dracula's spread of vampirism mirrors period fears of disease and foreign incursion
- **Faith and ritual as weapons** — crucifixes, holy wafers, and sanctified earth are as vital to the hunt as any weapon

## Key Takeaways
- The novel's epistolary structure (diaries, letters, phonograph recordings, newspaper clippings) builds suspense through fragmented, first-person documentation
- Lucy Westenra's transformation and staking is the novel's most graphic set piece, requiring all her former suitors' participation
- Dracula's native soil-filled boxes, which he must sleep in, are the practical key to tracking and trapping him
- Renfield's insect-eating and his psychic sensitivity to Dracula's presence make him an unwilling barometer of the Count's proximity
- Mina's psychic link to Dracula, forged when he forces her to drink his blood, is both her curse and the group's tracking tool
- The final chase from London back to Transylvania compresses the whole hunt into a race against Mina's impending transformation
- Comparison point: Dracula is a deliberately predatory, adult monster preying on victims by choice, contrasting with Frankenstein's Creature, a made being turned violent by rejection
