# The Hound of the Baskervilles — Arthur Conan Doyle

## Summary
The Hound of the Baskervilles (1901-02) is Arthur Conan Doyle's third Sherlock Holmes novel, blending detective fiction with Gothic horror on the fog-bound moors of Dartmoor. Dr. James Mortimer brings Holmes the legend of a spectral hound said to stalk the Baskerville family after Sir Charles Baskerville is found dead of apparent fright, gigantic paw prints near his body. When the heir, Sir Henry Baskerville, arrives from Canada and receives an anonymous warning, Holmes sends Dr. Watson to guard him at Baskerville Hall while secretly conducting his own investigation from hiding on the moor. Watson's narration, drawn largely from his letters and diary, builds an atmosphere of escaped convicts, moving lights, and a mournful howl before Holmes reveals the "curse" as an elaborate murder scheme using a real dog painted with phosphorus. It remains the most famous and frequently adapted Holmes novel, prized for its sustained suspense and moorland setting.

## Plot
Dr. James Mortimer consults Holmes about Sir Charles Baskerville's sudden death on the grounds of Baskerville Hall, his heart apparently stopped by fright, with the footprints of an enormous hound found nearby, feeding the old family legend of a spectral beast cursing the Baskervilles. Sir Henry Baskerville, the American-raised heir, arrives in London, receives an anonymous warning to stay off the moor, and has a boot stolen from his hotel. Holmes sends Watson to accompany Sir Henry to Baskerville Hall, while he himself secretly remains hidden on the moor conducting his own inquiry unknown even to Watson. Watson investigates the escaped convict Selden, hiding on the moor and fed secretly by the butler Barrymore (Selden's brother-in-law), and grows suspicious of the naturalist Jack Stapleton and his supposed sister Beryl, as well as Laura Lyons, who had arranged to meet Sir Charles the night he died. Selden is later found dead on the moor, killed by the hound while wearing Sir Henry's cast-off clothes. Holmes finally reveals Stapleton as a secret Baskerville heir who kept a genuine mastiff, coated in phosphorus to look ghostly, to murder his way to the inheritance. In the climax on the moor, Holmes, Watson, and Lestrade shoot the hound and Stapleton drowns fleeing through the treacherous Grimpen Mire.

## Characters
- **Sherlock Holmes** — the detective, who secretly investigates from a hut on the moor while appearing to leave the case to Watson
- **Dr. Watson** — narrator, sent to guard Sir Henry and gather evidence at Baskerville Hall
- **Sir Henry Baskerville** — the Canadian-raised heir to the cursed estate, the hound's intended final victim
- **Dr. James Mortimer** — the Baskerville family friend and doctor who first brings the case to Holmes
- **Jack Stapleton** — naturalist neighbor revealed as a hidden Baskerville heir and the murderer behind the hound
- **Beryl Stapleton** — Stapleton's wife, presented as his sister, coerced into helping and later freed
- **Barrymore and Mrs. Barrymore** — Baskerville Hall's butler and housekeeper, secretly aiding her convict brother Selden
- **Selden** — an escaped murderer hiding on the moor, mistakenly killed by the hound in Sir Henry's clothes
- **Laura Lyons** — a local woman manipulated by Stapleton, whose letter to Sir Charles proves crucial evidence

## Themes
- **Rational detection versus superstition** — Holmes insists on a material, evidentiary explanation for a supposedly supernatural curse
- **Gothic atmosphere** — Dartmoor's fog, the Grimpen Mire, and the convict on the moor sustain horror-novel dread throughout
- **Inheritance and greed** — Stapleton's murder plot is driven entirely by his hidden claim to the Baskerville fortune
- **Appearances and disguise** — Stapleton's false identity as Beryl's brother and as a harmless naturalist conceals the true threat
- **Isolation** — Watson's solitary vigil at Baskerville Hall heightens the reader's sense of vulnerability on the moor

## Key Takeaways
- The legend of a spectral hound cursing the Baskerville line since a 17th-century ancestor's cruelty frames the entire mystery
- Holmes secretly hides in a stone hut on the moor for much of the novel, observing events unknown to Watson
- Stapleton disguises a real mastiff with phosphorus paint to make it glow and appear supernatural in the dark
- The Grimpen Mire, a deadly bog on the moor, ultimately swallows Stapleton as he flees the final confrontation
- Selden, an escaped convict wearing Sir Henry's discarded clothes, is killed by the hound in a case of mistaken identity
- Laura Lyons's undelivered letter to Sir Charles becomes key evidence tying Stapleton to the fatal night
- Widely regarded as the best-known Holmes novel, it sits alongside the short-story collections as Doyle's most adapted work
