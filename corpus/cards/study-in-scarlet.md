# A Study in Scarlet — Arthur Conan Doyle

## Summary
A Study in Scarlet (1887) is Arthur Conan Doyle's first Sherlock Holmes novel, introducing the detective, Dr. John Watson, and their shared lodgings at 221B Baker Street. Watson, a former army doctor invalided home from Afghanistan, is introduced to Holmes by their mutual acquaintance Stamford and is soon startled by Holmes's uncanny deductions about his own history. The two are drawn into a murder investigation when Inspector Gregson and Inspector Lestrade seek Holmes's help with the death of Enoch Drebber, found in an empty house with the word "RACHE" written in blood on the wall, followed by the killing of his secretary Joseph Stangerson. The novel's second half breaks from London entirely for an extended flashback set among Mormon settlers in Utah, tracing the injustice that drove the killer, Jefferson Hope, to a years-long transatlantic pursuit of revenge, before returning to Holmes's final explanation of his method.

## Plot
Dr. Watson, newly returned from military service in Afghanistan, moves into 221B Baker Street with Sherlock Holmes, whose uncanny ability to deduce Watson's history from small details astonishes him. Inspectors Gregson and Lestrade call Holmes to investigate the murder of Enoch Drebber, found dead in an empty house with no visible wound and the word "RACHE" (German for revenge) written in blood on the wall. Holmes deduces the killer's height, means of transport, and manner from the scene, while the police pursue false leads. A second murder follows, that of Drebber's secretary Joseph Stangerson, stabbed in his hotel room. Holmes identifies and arrests the cab driver Jefferson Hope as the killer. The narrative then shifts entirely to years earlier in Utah, recounting how John Ferrier and his adopted daughter Lucy were rescued by Mormon settlers, how Lucy fell in love with Jefferson Hope, and how Mormon elders forced Lucy into a marriage that led to Ferrier's murder and Lucy's death. Hope, having tracked Drebber and Stangerson (the men responsible) to London over years, poisoned Drebber with one of two pills, one lethal and one harmless, forcing him to choose, and stabbed Stangerson when he resisted. Hope explains his motive to Holmes and Watson before dying of an aneurysm prior to trial.

## Characters
- **Sherlock Holmes** — the eccentric consulting detective, introduced here deducing Watson's Afghan service on first meeting
- **Dr. John Watson** — narrator, an army doctor wounded in Afghanistan who becomes Holmes's roommate and chronicler
- **Inspector Gregson and Inspector Lestrade** — rival Scotland Yard detectives who bring Holmes the Drebber murder case
- **Jefferson Hope** — the cab driver revealed as the killer, driven by years of grief and revenge
- **Enoch Drebber and Joseph Stangerson** — the two victims, former Mormon elders responsible for Lucy Ferrier's forced marriage and death
- **John Ferrier** — Utah settler who adopts Lucy, later killed for refusing to give her to a Mormon elder's son
- **Lucy Ferrier** — Ferrier's adopted daughter, in love with Jefferson Hope, dies after a forced marriage

## Themes
- **Deductive reasoning as method** — Holmes's opening demonstrations establish the observational method that defines the entire series
- **Revenge across time and distance** — Hope's years-long pursuit of Drebber and Stangerson structures the whole second half
- **Justice outside the law** — Hope's private vengeance is framed sympathetically despite being murder
- **Structural experiment** — the novel splits into a London detective story and a Utah frontier melodrama told in flashback

## Key Takeaways
- Holmes's very first deduction, that Watson has just returned from Afghanistan, remains one of the series' most quoted scenes
- The word "RACHE," German for revenge, scrawled in blood at the crime scene misleads police into suspecting a German culprit
- Hope's murder method uses two identical pills, one poisoned, forcing each victim to choose his own fate
- The lengthy Utah backstory about Mormon settlers and the Ferriers is Doyle's most extended digression in any Holmes story
- This novel establishes 221B Baker Street and the Holmes-Watson partnership that anchors the entire canon
- Hope dies of a pre-existing aneurysm just before he can be tried, closing the case without a courtroom scene
- Introduces Holmes's rivalry-tinged working relationship with official police in the persons of Gregson and Lestrade
