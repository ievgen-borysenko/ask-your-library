# Around the World in Eighty Days — Jules Verne

## Summary
Around the World in Eighty Days (1872) follows the meticulous, unflappable English gentleman Phileas Fogg, who wagers half his fortune at London's Reform Club that he can circumnavigate the globe in exactly eighty days using the new networks of railways and steamships. Accompanied by his newly hired French valet, Jean Passepartout, Fogg races through Suez, Bombay, Calcutta, Hong Kong, Yokohama, San Francisco, and New York, encountering obstacles at nearly every stage, from a burning suttee ritual in India, where they rescue the young widow Aouda, to train delays, storms, and the theft-obsessed pursuit of Detective Fix, who wrongly believes Fogg is the culprit behind a Bank of England robbery and dogs him across continents hoping to arrest him once in British territory. Verne's celebration of industrial-age travel and precise timetables culminates in a famous twist involving the International Date Line, resolving Fogg's apparent failure into a narrow, triumphant success, and secures his marriage to Aouda along the way.

## Plot
Phileas Fogg, a wealthy, exactingly punctual London gentleman, bets his fellow Reform Club members twenty thousand pounds that he can travel around the world in eighty days using the era's new steamship and railway networks. He departs immediately with his newly hired valet, Passepartout, unaware that Scotland Yard detective Fix is trailing him, suspecting Fogg of having robbed the Bank of England and hoping for a warrant to arrest him once he reaches British soil. The journey takes them through Suez, across India by elephant when a rail line proves incomplete, where they rescue a young widow, Aouda, from being forced onto her deceased husband's funeral pyre. They continue through Calcutta, Hong Kong, Yokohama (where Fogg is briefly separated from the drugged and delayed Passepartout), across the Pacific to San Francisco, and by train across America, surviving a buffalo herd delay, a Sioux attack, and a bridge collapse. Fix, having failed to secure an arrest warrant in time, eventually arrests Fogg in Liverpool near journey's end, delaying him just long enough that Fogg believes he has lost the wager by arriving in London a day late. In a final twist, Fogg realizes that by traveling eastward around the globe he has gained a full day across the International Date Line, arriving with time to spare. He wins the wager, and along the way has fallen in love with and proposes to Aouda.

## Characters
- **Phileas Fogg** — a wealthy, imperturbably precise London gentleman who wagers his fortune on an eighty-day circumnavigation
- **Passepartout** — Fogg's loyal, resourceful French valet, whose mishaps and quick thinking repeatedly affect the journey
- **Detective Fix** — a Scotland Yard detective who wrongly suspects Fogg of bank robbery and pursues him across the globe
- **Aouda** — a young Indian widow rescued from a forced suttee ritual, who later marries Fogg

## Themes
- **Precision and the industrial age** — Fogg's exact timetable embodies faith in the era's new railways, steamships, and telegraphs
- **Wrongful suspicion** — Fix's misguided pursuit of an innocent man drives much of the novel's tension and irony
- **Travel as spectacle** — the journey catalogues global customs and landscapes through a distinctly Victorian, imperial lens
- **Time as a hidden variable** — the International Date Line twist reframes the entire novel's stakes at the last moment

## Key Takeaways
- Fogg's wager of twenty thousand pounds at the Reform Club sets the entire eighty-day circumnavigation in motion
- The rescue of the widow Aouda from a forced suttee ritual in India is the novel's central act of moral heroism
- Detective Fix pursues Fogg across three continents in the mistaken belief that he robbed the Bank of England
- The famous twist ending reveals Fogg gained an extra day by traveling eastward across the International Date Line
- Passepartout's mishaps, including being drugged and left behind in Yokohama, repeatedly complicate the tight schedule
- The American leg includes a buffalo herd delay, a Sioux attack, and a collapsing bridge crossed just in time
- Comparison point: a globe-spanning adventure of ingenuity and endurance that sits alongside Verne's other journeys of exploration
